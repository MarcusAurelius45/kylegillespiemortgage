Buying a $1.5 Million or Higher-Priced Home in Lincoln Park or Winnetka: Jumbo Financing Step by Step

Short answer: Buying a home priced at $1.5 million or more in Lincoln Park or Winnetka may require a jumbo loan, depending on your down payment and loan amount. A jumbo loan exceeds the applicable conforming loan limit.

The process involves five steps: choosing a down payment, documenting income and reserves, reviewing the property, estimating taxes and closing costs, and coordinating the closing timeline. Having your income, assets, and credit reviewed before making an offer may help identify financing questions early.

Step 1: How much should I put down?

Jumbo down payment requirements vary by loan amount, property type, credit profile, and program. Larger loans may require a larger down payment or additional reserves.

Compare down payment options before committing funds. On a $1.8 million Lincoln Park purchase, a larger down payment may move the loan into a different financing tier, potentially affecting pricing or qualification requirements.

Kyle reviews current program requirements for your situation.

Step 2: What documentation may be required?

Depending on your income and program, an underwriter may request:

  • Tax returns, including schedules and Schedule K-1 forms reporting income from partnerships, corporations, or trusts.
  • Documentation of bonuses, commissions, or restricted stock units.
  • Bank and investment statements, plus documentation explaining large deposits.
  • Evidence of reserves, meaning eligible assets remaining after closing.

Reserve requirements vary and may be measured in months of housing expenses, including principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, and applicable association dues. Eligible retirement and investment accounts may count at a reduced value.

Review reserves before choosing your down payment.

Step 3: How does the property affect financing?

Lincoln Park: Single-family homes, condominiums, and cooperative properties may have different financing requirements. Condominiums may require a review of the building's financial condition, insurance, and other eligibility factors. Cooperative financing availability varies by program.

Winnetka: Distinctive homes, larger lots, or properties with limited comparable sales may require additional appraisal review.

For either market, discuss renovations early. Financing options may depend on the property's condition and the scope of work. Historic designation or local permitting requirements may also affect renovation plans.

Step 4: How do taxes and closing costs affect the budget?

Property taxes, insurance, and applicable association dues affect both your monthly budget and mortgage qualification.

For new construction or substantially renovated homes, the current tax bill may not reflect the property's future assessment. Request an estimate before setting your price ceiling.

Transfer taxes and other closing costs vary by location and transaction. Review your Loan Estimate with your lender and discuss tax prorations with your attorney. Actual costs may differ from initial estimates.

Step 5: What does the closing timeline involve?

  1. Before making an offer: Have your income, assets, and credit reviewed. Pre-approval remains subject to applicable conditions and final underwriting approval.
  2. Contract review: Coordinate attorney review, financing deadlines, and tax prorations with your real estate agent and attorney. Deadlines depend on the contract.
  3. Appraisal: Coordinate the appraisal with your lender. Additional valuations or property reviews may affect timing.
  4. Final approval and closing: Updated documents, reserve verification, and title review may be required. If proceeds from another home sale will fund the purchase, discuss that dependency early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the same conforming loan limit apply in Lincoln Park and Winnetka?

Both are in Cook County. For the same property unit count and applicable year, the same county conforming loan limit applies. Whether jumbo financing is needed depends on the loan amount.

May bonuses and restricted stock units count as income?

They may qualify if they meet the program's documentation, history, and continuance requirements. The qualifying amount may differ from your current compensation.

Do jumbo loans require private mortgage insurance?

Some jumbo programs may offer financing without separate private mortgage insurance. Down payment, reserve, and other requirements vary by program.

May I purchase with cash and obtain a mortgage afterward?

Delayed financing may be available through certain programs, subject to eligibility, documentation, timing, and loan limits. Discuss the requirements before purchasing.
