Buying a $1.5M+ Home in Lincoln Park or Winnetka: Jumbo Financing Step by Step

Short answer: A $1.5 million or larger purchase in Lincoln Park or Winnetka almost always means a jumbo loan, because both sit in Cook County, where the conforming limit is the national baseline. The jumbo process for a Chicago buyer runs in five steps: set the down payment against the program's loan-size tiers, document income and post-closing reserves, match the financing to the property type, budget Cook County taxes and transfer taxes, and build the Illinois contract timeline around the appraisal. Getting fully underwritten before you write an offer is the single step that most changes how smoothly the rest goes.

Kyle Gillespie, SVP of Mortgage Lending at OriginPoint (a Rate company), NMLS #223778, has financed North Side and North Shore homes for 20+ years and structures jumbo purchases in Lincoln Park and Winnetka every season.

Step 1: How much should I put down on a $1.5M home in Lincoln Park or Winnetka?

Jumbo programs price and qualify by loan-size tier. A loan just above the conforming limit typically has the most flexible terms; requirements tighten as the balance passes roughly $1.5 million, and again around $2 million and $3 million. In practice, many primary-residence jumbo programs start in the 10% to 20% down range at moderate balances, and larger loans often call for 20% to 30% or more. These tiers vary by investor and change over time, so Kyle confirms the current figures for your loan amount.

Model the loan at the edge of each tier. On a $1.8 million Lincoln Park purchase, a slightly larger down payment that drops the loan into a lower tier may help more than the extra cash suggests.

Step 2: What does a jumbo underwriter need to see?

A human underwriter reads jumbo files against an investor's written guidelines. Expect to provide:

  • Two years of complete tax returns, including all schedules and any K-1s.
  • A history for variable pay such as bonuses, commissions, and restricted stock units.
  • Two to three months of statements for every account funding the purchase, with large deposits sourced.
  • Post-closing reserves. Commonly 6 to 12 months of the full housing payment, sometimes more at larger balances. Retirement and brokerage accounts often count at a discounted value, not the full balance.

Reserves are where well-qualified Chicago buyers most often fall short: the down payment clears, then what remains does not meet the reserve test. Count reserves before choosing a down payment.

Step 3: How does the property type change the financing?

The two markets share a loan limit but not housing stock, and the house shapes the file.

Lincoln Park

Much of the $1.5M+ inventory is new construction or gut-rehabbed single-family homes on 25-foot city lots, plus vintage greystones and rowhouses. Blocks inside landmark districts such as Mid-North and Arlington-Deming need Commission on Chicago Landmarks review for exterior changes, which affects renovation timelines, not loan eligibility. Lakefront high-rises add a building review on top of yours, and some vintage lakefront buildings are cooperatives, which far fewer jumbo investors finance. Confirm co-op eligibility before you tour; for condos, see Fannie Mae's August 2026 Condo Rules.

Winnetka

Winnetka inventory leans toward larger lots, older center-entry colonials and Tudors, and teardown-to-new-build projects. Distinctive homes on large lots mean fewer close comparable sales, so appraisals take longer and some programs require a second appraisal or desk review at higher balances. If the house needs work, ask early whether renovation costs could be financed in the jumbo loan or whether you should plan to fund them separately.

Step 4: How do Cook County taxes affect a jumbo approval?

Lincoln Park and Winnetka both pay Cook County property taxes, billed a year in arrears. The Assessor reassesses the City of Chicago and the north suburbs in different years of its three-year cycle, so the two markets reset on different schedules. Underwriters qualify you on a forward-looking estimate, and on a newly built home the seller's bill may reflect a partial-year or pre-construction assessment far below what you will pay. Get a realistic estimate before setting your price ceiling.

Cash to close differs too. In Chicago, the buyer pays the city's share of the real property transfer tax, which is unusual nationally; North Shore municipalities set their own rules. Your Loan Estimate itemizes these charges.

Step 5: What does the timeline look like from offer to closing?

  1. Before you shop: full underwriting of income, assets, and credit, so your pre-approval reflects a reviewed file, not just a credit pull.
  2. Offer and attorney review: Illinois is an attorney state. Under the standard Chicago-area contract, attorney review typically runs five business days, and your attorney negotiates the mortgage contingency and tax proration.
  3. Appraisal: order it immediately; a second valuation on a unique home may add a week or more.
  4. Final approval and closing: updated statements, reserve verification, and title work. If a home sale funds the purchase, the contract and approval need to reflect it.

FAQ

Does a Winnetka purchase fall under the same loan limit as Lincoln Park?

Yes. Winnetka and Lincoln Park are both in Cook County, so the same conforming loan limit applies to both, and any loan above it is jumbo. The difference between the two markets shows up in property type, appraisal, and taxes, not in the limit itself.

Do restricted stock units and bonuses count as income on a jumbo loan?

Often, if they are documented. Underwriters generally want a history of vesting or payment, usually around two years, plus evidence the income is likely to continue. A first-year bonus or a one-time grant is commonly excluded or averaged down, so the qualifying figure may be lower than the offer letter suggests.

Is private mortgage insurance part of a jumbo loan?

Usually not. Most jumbo programs manage risk through larger down payments and reserve requirements rather than mortgage insurance. That is one reason the equity requirement is higher than on a conforming loan.

Could I pay cash for a Lincoln Park home and add a mortgage after closing?

Possibly. Fannie Mae's delayed financing exception lets a buyer who paid cash take a cash-out refinance within six months of purchase, subject to conditions and the conforming limit, and some jumbo programs offer a similar path above that limit. The purchase funds have to be documented, so plan the paper trail before closing.
