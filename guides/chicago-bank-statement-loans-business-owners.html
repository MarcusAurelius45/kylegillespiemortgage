Bank-Statement Loans for Chicago Business Owners: An Alternative Way to Document Income

Short answer: A bank-statement loan may allow a Chicago business owner to qualify using eligible deposits on personal or business bank statements rather than tax-return income. These loans are often offered through non-qualified mortgage programs, which use underwriting criteria that differ from qualified mortgages.

This option may help self-employed borrowers whose business deductions reduce taxable income. Income verification is still required, and not every deposit counts as qualifying income. Compare available bank-statement and conventional financing options before choosing a program.

Why do Chicago business owners consider bank-statement loans?

Conventional self-employed financing generally relies on tax returns, with adjustments for certain expenses. For some business owners, the resulting qualifying income may limit their financing options.

A restaurant owner in Logan Square, a contractor in Portage Park, or a self-employed real estate agent may have business deductions that reduce taxable income. A bank-statement program may provide another way to evaluate income through documented deposits, adjusted for business expenses.

How do deposits become qualifying income?

An underwriter reviews eligible deposits over the required statement period. Transfers between accounts, borrowed funds, and other deposits that do not represent qualifying income may be excluded.

For business accounts, the lender may apply an expense factor to estimate income after business expenses. Depending on the program, this may use a standard percentage, a profit and loss statement, or documentation from a certified public accountant.

Personal and business statements may be treated differently. Business ownership, statement history, credit, down payment, and reserve requirements vary by program.

What documents may be required?

  • Complete bank statements, including every page, for the required review period.
  • Verification of business ownership and operating history, such as business registration records, applicable licenses, or acceptable third-party documentation.
  • A profit and loss statement or expense documentation, if required.
  • Explanations and supporting records for large or unusual deposits.
  • Statements documenting down payment funds and required reserves, meaning eligible assets remaining after closing.

Some programs may also request tax returns or tax transcripts. Confirm documentation requirements before applying.

What may complicate a bank-statement loan?

Mixed personal and business activity. Combining personal funds and business revenue may make deposits harder to verify. Unclear deposits may be excluded.

Undeposited cash. Cash sales or tips that are not deposited generally cannot be counted through a bank-statement analysis.

Seasonal or declining revenue. A seasonal business may need additional documentation explaining income fluctuations or recent declines.

Property eligibility. Condominiums, properties with two to four units, investment properties, and larger loan amounts may have additional requirements. For a condominium, both the borrower and the building may need to meet program guidelines.

How does it compare with conventional financing?

Neither approach is automatically better. A conventional loan may offer more favorable pricing if documented tax-return income supports qualification. A bank-statement loan may provide an alternative, but rates, fees, down payments, and reserves may differ.

Compare the total cost and terms of each available option. Property taxes, insurance, and applicable association dues also affect the housing payment and qualification.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a bank-statement loan the same as a stated-income loan?

No. A bank-statement loan requires documented income analysis rather than simply accepting the income stated on an application. Consumer mortgages subject to federal ability-to-repay requirements also require the lender to evaluate repayment ability.

May I use one for a two-flat or investment property?

Some programs may allow properties with two to four units or investment purchases. Eligibility and the treatment of rental income vary by program. Business-purpose investment loans may follow different requirements from consumer home loans.

May I refinance into a conventional loan later?

You may qualify if your income documentation, credit, property, and other factors meet the requirements at that time. Future approval, rates, and savings are not guaranteed. Refinancing also involves costs.

Do I need a separate business bank account?

Not always. Some programs may accept personal statements containing business deposits. A separate business account may make revenue and expenses easier to document.
