Bank-Statement Loans for Chicago Business Owners: Qualifying Without W-2s

Short answer: A bank-statement loan is a non-qualified mortgage (non-QM) program that may let a Chicago business owner qualify using the deposits on personal or business bank statements instead of W-2s or the net income on tax returns. It is built for owners whose write-offs lower taxable income below what the business actually brings in.

The lender still verifies your ability to repay; it simply uses deposit history as the evidence. Terms may differ from conventional financing, so the honest first step is to see how your file reads under both approaches.

Why do Chicago business owners look at bank-statement loans?

Conventional underwriting starts with your tax returns. Fannie Mae's guidelines direct lenders to analyze self-employed income from those returns, adding back certain non-cash items such as depreciation. For many Chicago owners, the result still looks thin on paper.

Think of a restaurant owner in Logan Square depreciating a new kitchen build-out, a general contractor in Portage Park writing off trucks and equipment, or a real estate agent paid on 1099s with heavy marketing expenses. Each may run a healthy business while reporting modest taxable income. A bank-statement program looks at money moving through the accounts instead.

How does a lender turn deposits into qualifying income?

The underwriter totals eligible deposits across the review period, then removes anything that is not business revenue: transfers between your own accounts, loan or line-of-credit proceeds, refunds, and one-time items such as the sale of a vehicle.

Because deposits are gross revenue, the lender then applies an expense factor to estimate what the business keeps. That factor may be a program default or may be based on a profit and loss (P&L) statement or a letter from your certified public accountant (CPA). Personal and business statements may be treated differently. The review period, expense method, credit standards, and down payment all vary by loan amount, property type, credit profile, and program.

What documents does a Chicago bank-statement file need?

  • Complete monthly statements, every page, for each account used to calculate income.
  • Proof the business exists and that you own it, such as Illinois Secretary of State records for a limited liability company (LLC) or corporation, a CPA letter, and, where applicable, a City of Chicago business license from the Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection.
  • A P&L statement or CPA letter if the program uses one to set the expense factor.
  • Written explanations for large or unusual deposits.
  • Asset statements showing down payment funds and any required reserves.

Whether a program also asks for tax transcripts varies, so confirm before you assume returns are off the table.

What trips up bank-statement files in Chicago?

Commingled accounts. When personal spending and business revenue share one account, the underwriter has to sort every deposit, and unclear items may be excluded.

Cash that never hits the bank. Only deposited revenue counts. Tips or cash sales kept out of the account do not show up as income.

Seasonality. A Wrigleyville bar with a strong patio season, a landscaper, or a snow-removal operator may show large swings by month. Underwriters may ask about a recent decline even when the annual total looks fine.

The property itself. Some programs follow condo project standards closely. If you are buying in a high-rise, review how the August 2026 condo rules affect the building, and know that some non-QM programs may finance non-warrantable condos. Larger loans may also cross into jumbo territory, which carries its own program rules.

How does a bank-statement loan compare with conventional self-employed financing?

Neither path is automatically better. If your tax returns support the payment, a conventional loan may offer lower costs and broader property eligibility. If they do not, a bank-statement loan may be the route that reflects your actual cash flow, usually with different pricing and terms, and some programs may include prepayment provisions.

Either way, Cook County property taxes, insurance, and any association dues count in your debt-to-income ratio. On a Chicago 2-flat or a single-family home in North Center, a tax bill that jumps after reassessment may change how much the deposits need to support.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a bank-statement loan the same as a stated-income loan?

No. Federal ability-to-repay rules require lenders to verify income using reliable third-party records. Bank statements are that verification; the lender does not simply accept the income you state on the application.

May I use a bank-statement loan for a 2-flat or an investment property?

Some programs may allow two- to four-unit properties and investment purchases, while others limit eligibility to a primary residence. Projected rental income may or may not count, depending on the program and the appraisal.

May I refinance into a conventional loan later?

Possibly, if your tax returns later support the income a conventional loan requires and the property and credit still qualify. Check the note for any prepayment provision before planning a refinance.

Do I need a separate business bank account?

Not always. Some programs accept personal statements that carry business deposits. A dedicated business account usually makes deposits easier to trace and explain, which may shorten underwriting questions.
