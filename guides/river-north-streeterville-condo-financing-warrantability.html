Condo Financing in River North and Streeterville: High-Rise Buildings and Warrantability

Short answer: Condo financing in River North and Streeterville depends on the building as much as the buyer. For loan applications dated on or after August 3, 2026, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac require a Full Review of nearly every condominium project with more than 10 units, so a lender examines a high-rise's budget, reserves, insurance, litigation, and commercial space on every conventional loan.

Towers that pass are considered warrantable. Towers that operate like a hotel, contain significant commercial space, or face unresolved structural repairs may not be. Starting the building review before the offer gives a buyer time to learn which financing paths are open.

Why do River North and Streeterville high-rises get extra scrutiny?

Both neighborhoods are dominated by large mixed-use towers: glass-and-concrete buildings with retail at street level, multi-level parking podiums, amenity floors, and in some cases a hotel sharing the same structure. River North adds converted loft buildings, former warehouses now divided into condominiums. Streeterville's towers cluster near the lakefront, Navy Pier, and the Michigan Avenue corridor.

Each of those features maps to a question in the agency project review: how much of the building is nonresidential, who controls the commercial and parking units, how the master insurance policy is structured, and whether the building operates anything that resembles a hotel. A three-flat in Lakeview rarely raises several of these questions at once. A downtown tower often does.

What makes a downtown Chicago condo building non-warrantable?

Agency guidance lists project characteristics that may make a building ineligible for conventional financing. Those most relevant to downtown high-rises:

  • Hotel-like operations: a rental desk, short-term rental programs, mandatory rental pooling, or other features that resemble a hotel or condotel.
  • Commercial space: nonresidential space above agency limits, including retail and garage units owned separately from the residential association.
  • Single-entity ownership: one developer, investor, or affiliated group holding too many units, which may occur in newer towers still selling out.
  • Litigation: lawsuits involving the association, particularly those concerning structural or safety issues.
  • Critical repairs: unfunded or incomplete structural, facade, or other major repair work.
  • Insurance: master policy coverage and deductible requirements, including the per-unit deductible limit that applies to applications on or after July 1, 2026.

Did the 2026 rule changes help or hurt downtown condo buyers?

Both. Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03, issued March 18, 2026, retired the investor-concentration limit for established projects reviewed under Full Review, effective on that date. Rental-heavy towers that were previously ineligible for conventional investment-property loans may now qualify, provided they pass every other part of the review.

At the same time, the Government-Sponsored Enterprises retired Fannie Mae's Limited Review and Freddie Mac's Streamlined Review for projects with more than 10 units, so a larger down payment no longer shortens the building review. For loan applications on or after January 4, 2027, the minimum replacement reserve allocation also rises. See Fannie Mae's August 2026 Condo Rules and the January 2027 reserve rule guide for details.

What should a River North or Streeterville buyer request before making an offer?

  • The lender condo questionnaire, completed by the management company.
  • The current association budget and the most recent reserve study.
  • The master insurance certificate, including the deductible.
  • Recent board meeting minutes, which may reveal planned facade, window, elevator, or garage projects.
  • Disclosure of pending litigation and any special assessments.
  • Confirmation of whether a parking space is a separately deeded unit with its own assessment.

The Illinois Condominium Property Act requires a unit seller to make certain association documents and disclosures available to a prospective buyer on request, including information about reserves, capital expenditures, pending lawsuits, and insurance. Ask how long management takes to return a lender questionnaire and build that time into the contract.

What happens if a River North or Streeterville building does not pass?

A non-warrantable building does not end the conversation. Portfolio and non-qualified mortgage programs evaluate the building under their own standards, and their terms differ from conventional financing. If the loan amount exceeds the conforming loan limit, a jumbo lender applies its own condo review as well; see Jumbo Loan Limits in Chicago 2026.

A building may also regain eligibility once it resolves the issue that failed the review. For more, see Financing a Non-Warrantable Condo in Chicago and Why Your Loan Got Denied on the Building, Not You. Requirements vary by loan amount, property type, credit profile, and program. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a building's approval in Fannie Mae's Condo Project Manager mean my loan is approved?

No. Condo Project Manager is a tool lenders use to evaluate and document a project's eligibility. Project eligibility is separate from borrower approval, so the buyer's credit, income, assets, and the unit itself are still underwritten.

Do newer towers that are still selling units face different rules?

New or newly converted projects may be reviewed under requirements that differ from those for established projects. Units still held by the developer may also count toward single-entity ownership limits until they sell.

Which date decides whether the 2026 condo rules apply to my purchase?

The agencies tie the changes to the loan application date, not the contract or closing date. A buyer who applies before or after a change date may face different project requirements, so confirm the application date with the lender.

Should sellers in River North and Streeterville check warrantability before listing?

It may help. Gathering the lender questionnaire, budget, reserve study, and insurance certificate before listing may shorten the buyer's timeline and reveal a building issue while there is still time to address it.
